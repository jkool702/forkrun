# Fork audit — experimental distributed-ingest engine

The experimental engine is **threads-only**: it spawns and joins worker and
consumer threads via `pthread_create`/`pthread_join`. It makes **no fork(),
exec(), or other process-replacement calls** (verified by grep across
`stage0/dist_ingest/dist_ingest.c` and `stage0/dist_ingest/*.h`; zero
matches). It never calls `system()`, `popen()`, or socket APIs either —
it is a single-process memory-fence/IO engine.

## Why this matters

A thread-only engine has no fork semantics hazards:
- No orphaned children or zombie reaping paths (nothing to reap).
- No `fork()` vs-threads deadlock concerns (the Python frontend had this,
  the engine never needed to).
- No cleanup of a child process subtree on teardown — `di_engine_destroy`
  joins threads only (`pthread_join`, `dist_ingest.c:1350/1360`).
